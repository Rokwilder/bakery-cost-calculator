How to Price Decorated Sugar Cookies

Find your real cost per custom cookie and a fair price per dozen.

Free Recipe Cost Calculator › Pricing guides

Decorated sugar cookies are a completely different product from drop cookies. The dough is cheap, but every cookie gets outlined, flooded, dried and detailed by hand, often over two days. If you price them like chocolate chip cookies, you'll work for almost nothing. This guide shows how to price decorated sugar cookies with royal icing, with a worked example for 24 custom cookies.

The formula: (ingredients + labor + overhead + packaging) ÷ number of cookies = cost per cookie. Add your profit markup to get your price.

Step 1: Cost the dough and the royal icing

For each ingredient, divide what you used by the package size and multiply by the package price. Royal icing (powdered sugar and meringue powder) usually costs more than the cookie dough. The package prices below are example prices; use your own receipts.

Step 2: Time the decorating, not just the baking

Track a real set: mixing and chilling dough, rolling and cutting, baking, cooling, mixing and coloring icing, filling bags, outlining, flooding, drying time between layers, detail work, heat-sealing bags and cleanup. In this example that's 5 hours at $18 an hour, or $90.00 of labor for a simple design in 3–4 colors. Detailed designs, lettering, many colors or hand-painting can take twice as long, so time each design style separately.

Step 3: Overhead and packaging

Use 5–15% of ingredients plus labor for oven power, equipment wear and small supplies. At 10% that's $10.01. For packaging and decorating supplies, heat-seal bags for 24 cookies (about 10¢ each), a box, and disposable piping bags run about $5.60 here. Custom tags, ribbon or gift boxes add more.

Step 5: Add profit and set your price

Custom cookies are design work, so a higher markup makes sense. With a 40% markup, the example comes to $6.75 per cookie, or about $81.03 a dozen. Rounded, that's about $6.75 a cookie or $81 a dozen for a simple custom set. Basic designs with one or two colors can come in lower; detailed sets should be priced higher.

Pricing tips for decorated sugar cookies

  • Price in tiers, for example simple (1–2 colors), standard (3–4 colors) and detailed (lettering, many colors, hand-painting), so customers can pick a budget.
  • Set a minimum order, like one dozen per design. Mixing colors for 3 cookies takes nearly as long as for 24.
  • Charge extra for custom cutters, logos and matching a party theme exactly.
  • Book well ahead. Decorated cookies need drying time, so a rush order costs you a second evening. Add a rush fee.
  • Take a deposit when the order is booked, since custom cookies can't be resold to someone else.
