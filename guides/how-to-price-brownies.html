How to Price Brownies

Find your cost per brownie and a fair price per pan.

Free Recipe Cost Calculator › Pricing guides

Brownies are one of the best-selling items for home bakers: one pan, one bake, easy to cut and bag, and they travel well to markets. That makes them a great product, but only if your price covers the chocolate, butter, bags and your time. Here's a simple way to price brownies with a worked example for a 9×13 pan cut into 24 squares.

The formula: (ingredients + labor + overhead + packaging) ÷ number of brownies = cost per brownie. Add your profit markup to get your price.

Step 1: Add up your ingredients

Divide the amount you used by the package size, then multiply by the package price. These are example prices; swap in what you actually pay.

Ingredient total: $8.85 for 24 brownies, about $0.37 each. Butter, cocoa and chocolate are most of the cost, so if you use premium chocolate, or add nuts, caramel or a frosting layer, cost that version separately.

Step 2: Count your time

Brownies are fast, but not free. Include mixing, baking, cooling fully before cutting, cutting clean squares, bagging, labeling and cleanup. In this example that's 1.25 hours at $18 an hour, or $22.50 of labor.

Step 3: Overhead and packaging

At 10% of ingredients plus labor for oven power, foil or parchment and equipment wear, overhead is $3.14. If you sell brownies individually wrapped, packaging adds up: at about 15¢ per bag and label, that's $3.60 for 24.

Step 5: Add profit and set your price

With a 30% markup, the example comes to $2.06 per brownie, or $49.52 for the full pan of 24. Rounded, that's about $2.00 each (or $2.25 if you'd rather round up), $12 for a half dozen, or around $50 for a whole pan.

Pricing tips for brownies

  • Decide your cut size first. The same pan cut into 12 large squares or 24 small ones gives a very different price per piece. Pick a size and stick to it.
  • Sell whole pans and boxes, not just singles. A whole pan saves you bagging time, so you can offer a small discount and still earn more.
  • Charge more for upgrades like frosted, swirled, stuffed or nut-topped versions, which take longer and cost more.
  • Bundle at markets (for example 3 for a set price) to keep the math easy and increase the average sale.
  • Re-cost when chocolate and butter prices change. They're the bulk of your cost.
