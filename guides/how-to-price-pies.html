How to Price Pies

Work out your real cost per pie and a fair price per pie or slice.

Free Recipe Cost Calculator › Pricing guides

Pie season is the busiest time of year for a lot of home bakers, and pies are easy to underprice. The filling alone can cost more than people expect, a from-scratch crust takes real time, and customers usually keep the pan. This guide shows how to price homemade pies so each one pays for your ingredients, your hours and your profit, with a worked example for two double-crust apple pies.

The formula: (ingredients + labor + overhead + packaging) ÷ number of pies = cost per pie. Add your profit markup to get your price.

Step 1: Cost the crust and the filling

For each ingredient, divide what you used by the package size and multiply by the package price. In a fruit pie, the fruit and the butter are most of the cost. The package prices below are example prices; use your own receipts, and re-check fruit prices in season and out.

Step 2: Count your time, crust included

Hands-on time includes making and chilling the dough, peeling and slicing the fruit, rolling, filling, crimping or latticing, egg-washing, baking, cooling, boxing and cleanup. In this example that's 2.5 hours for two pies at $18 an hour, or $45.00 of labor. Making four or six pies at once is much faster per pie than making one, which is a good reason to bake pies in batches.

Step 3: Overhead and packaging

Use 5–15% of ingredients plus labor for oven power, equipment wear and small supplies. At 10% that's $6.24. For packaging, a disposable foil or paper pie pan plus a pie box and label runs about $2.25 per pie, or $4.50 for two. If you bake in your own glass or ceramic dishes, either use disposable pans for orders or charge a refundable pan deposit.

Step 5: Add profit and set your price

With a 30% markup, the example comes to $47.53 per pie, or about $5.94 a slice if you cut each pie into 8. Rounded, that's about $48 for a 9-inch double-crust apple pie, or $6 a slice. If that's more than your local market will pay, look at labor first: it's well over half the cost here, and baking four or six pies in one session cuts the time per pie a lot. Don't fix it by cutting your hourly rate. A grocery-store pie is a different product from a homemade all-butter pie. If you sell by the slice at a market, charge a little more per slice than the whole-pie math to cover the extra handling and packaging.

Pricing tips for pies

  • Price each pie type on its own. Pecan, apple, cherry and pumpkin have very different ingredient costs.
  • Set holiday pre-order deadlines (for example, the Sunday before Thanksgiving) so you can buy fruit and butter once and bake in batches.
  • Charge for upgrades like lattice or decorative tops, crumb toppings, deep-dish pans and gluten-free crust.
  • Take deposits on holiday orders, since you buy ingredients days ahead.
  • Check your cottage food rules. Fruit pies are commonly allowed, but many states don't allow cream, custard or other pies that need refrigeration. Check before you put pumpkin or cream pies on your menu.
