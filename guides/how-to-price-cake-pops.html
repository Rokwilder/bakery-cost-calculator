How to Price Cake Pops

Find your real cost per cake pop and a fair price per dozen.

Free Recipe Cost Calculator › Pricing guides

Cake pops look small, so it's tempting to price them like a cookie. But they are one of the most hands-on things a home baker makes. You bake a cake, crumble it, mix and roll every ball, chill them, dip each one, decorate, let them set and wrap them one by one. The ingredients are cheap; the time is not. This guide shows how to price cake pops so your hours are actually paid, with a worked example for a batch of 48.

The formula: (ingredients + labor + overhead + packaging) ÷ number of cake pops = cost per pop. Add your profit markup to get your price.

Step 1: Cost the cake, binder and coating

Divide what you used by the package size, then multiply by the package price. The coating is usually the biggest ingredient cost, and it's easy to underestimate because you need extra melted chocolate in the cup to dip cleanly. Ingredient total: $16.66 for 48 cake pops, about $0.35 each. That's why cake pops feel like pure profit at $2 each. They aren't, once you count the next step.

Step 2: Time every stage

Track a real batch from start to finish: baking and cooling the cake, crumbling and mixing, rolling 48 even balls, chilling, dipping sticks, dipping and tapping off each pop, decorating, drying, bagging and tying, and cleanup. In this example that's 4 hours at $18 an hour, or $72.00 of labor. Custom designs like faces, characters, drizzles or hand-piped details can easily double that, so time them separately.

Step 3: Overhead and packaging

Use 5–15% of ingredients plus labor for oven power, equipment wear and small supplies such as parchment and foam blocks for drying. At 10% that's $8.87. Most cake pops are sold individually wrapped: a treat bag plus a ribbon or twist tie runs roughly 15¢, so 48 pops is $7.20. A display stand or gift box adds more.

Labor is about 70% of that cost. The real way to earn more on cake pops is to get faster, not to buy cheaper candy melts.

Step 5: Add profit and set your price

With a 30% markup, the example comes to $2.84 per cake pop, or about $34.04 a dozen. Rounded, that's about $2.85 each or $34 a dozen for simple dipped-and-sprinkled pops. Character or themed designs should be priced higher, because they take longer per pop.

Pricing tips for cake pops

  • Sell by the dozen with a minimum order. Setting up to dip 6 pops takes almost as long as dipping 24.
  • Price designs in tiers, for example basic dipped, drizzled or two-tone, and custom characters, so customers can choose a budget.
  • Charge for color matching. Custom-colored coating means extra candy melts and extra time.
  • Add a fee for individual tags, stands or gift boxes instead of absorbing them.
  • Take a deposit on party orders, since you buy and bake days ahead.
