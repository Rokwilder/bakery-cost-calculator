How to Price Cinnamon Rolls

Work out your cost per roll and a fair price per pan.

Free Recipe Cost Calculator › Pricing guides

Cinnamon rolls are a great seller for home bakers, especially for weekend pre-orders and holiday mornings. They also take most of a morning, between mixing, two rises, rolling, baking and icing. This guide shows how to price cinnamon rolls fairly, with a worked example for a pan of 12 classic rolls with cream cheese icing.

The formula: (ingredients + labor + overhead + packaging) ÷ number of rolls = cost per roll. Add your profit markup to get your price.

Step 1: Cost the dough, filling and icing

For each ingredient, divide what you used by the package size and multiply by the package price. Butter shows up in both the dough and the filling, and the cream cheese icing adds more than people expect. The package prices below are example prices; use your own receipts.

Step 2: Count your hands-on time (and decide about the rise)

Hands-on work includes mixing and kneading, rolling out, spreading the filling, cutting, panning, baking, making the icing, icing, packaging and cleanup. In this example that's 2 hours at $18 an hour, or $36.00 of labor.

Rising time is a judgment call. If you can do other paid baking while the dough rises, you can leave it out. If the rise ties you to the kitchen, for example on an early-morning bake for fresh pickup, count at least part of it. Whatever you decide, apply it the same way every time.

Step 3: Overhead and packaging

Use 5–15% of ingredients plus labor for oven power, equipment wear and small supplies. At 10% that's $4.37. For packaging, two foil pans with lids (6 rolls each) and labels run about $3.00. If you sell rolls individually, clamshell containers cost more per roll, so add that.

Step 5: Add profit and set your price

With a 30% markup, the example comes to $5.54 per roll, or $33.22 for a pan of 6. Rounded, that's about $5.50 a roll, $33 for a half dozen, or $66 for a dozen. Bigger bakery-style rolls cost more to make, so if yours are oversized, your batch yields fewer rolls and each one needs a higher price.

Pricing tips for cinnamon rolls

  • Sell by the pan. Half-dozen and dozen pans are easier to bake, package and price than single rolls.
  • Offer take-and-bake pans. Unbaked rolls save you oven and icing time and let customers serve them warm. Check that your state's cottage food rules allow it before you offer it.
  • Charge for add-ons like extra icing cups, pecans or caramel.
  • Use pre-order deadlines for weekend or holiday mornings so you bake exactly what's sold.
  • Re-cost when butter and cream cheese prices change. They're a big share of your cost.
