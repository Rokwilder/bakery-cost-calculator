How to Price Cakes

Work out your total cake cost, a price per serving, and what to charge for extras.

Free Recipe Cost Calculator › Pricing guides

Cakes are where home bakers lose the most money, because a single cake can take an entire afternoon. Baking the layers is the quick part; leveling, filling, crumb-coating, chilling, frosting and decorating are where the hours go. This guide shows a simple way to price a cake so the whole job pays you, using a worked example for a two-layer 8-inch buttercream cake.

The formula: ingredients + labor + overhead + packaging = cake cost. Add your profit markup to get the price. Divide by servings to get a price per serving you can reuse for any size.

Step 1: Cost the cake and the frosting

For each ingredient, divide what you used by the package size and multiply by the package price. Don't forget the buttercream: on a frosted cake it often costs as much as the cake itself. The package prices below are example prices; use your own receipts.

Step 2: Time the whole job, not just the bake

Count baking, cooling, leveling, filling, crumb coat, chilling, final frosting, decorating, boxing and cleanup, plus the time you spend messaging the customer about the design. In this example that's 4 hours at $18 an hour, or $72.00 of labor. A simple buttercream cake might take less; a carved, stacked or hand-painted cake can take a full day or more. Time a real cake before you quote one.

Step 3: Overhead and packaging

Use 5–15% of ingredients plus labor for oven power, equipment wear, parchment, piping bags and other small supplies. At 10%, that's $8.68. Packaging for a cake means at least a cake board and a cake box; we'll use $5.00. Tall or tiered cakes need taller boxes, dowels and support boards, so price those separately.

How many servings a cake yields depends on how it's cut, so decide on your own serving size, write it down, and use it every time you quote.

Step 5: Add profit and round

With a 30% markup, the example cake comes to $130.62, or about $8.16 per serving. You might round to $130. Custom designs, short-notice orders and wedding work usually justify a higher markup because they carry more design time and more risk.

Use a per-serving price for every size

Once you know your price per serving for a style of cake, quoting gets fast: multiply it by the number of servings the customer needs. Then add design extras on top. A common setup is a base per-serving price for a simple buttercream cake, with separate add-ons for:

  • Design work: piping, drips, florals, hand-painting, sculpted or carved shapes.
  • Premium flavors and fillings: fruit curds, ganache, cream cheese, liqueurs.
  • Toppers and decorations you buy in, at cost plus your markup.
  • Delivery and setup, priced by distance, especially for tiered cakes.
  • Rush orders placed with less than your normal notice.

Protect yourself on custom orders

Take a deposit when the order is booked so you're covered if the customer cancels after you've bought ingredients, set an order deadline, and write the design, flavors, servings and pickup time on an order form so there are no surprises. Check what other custom cake makers near you charge, but remember that a supermarket sheet cake isn't the same product as a made-to-order cake.
